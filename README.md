I developed an AI-powered Airport Operational Disruption Assistant that combines four analytical methods to identify, predict, and explore potential airport disruptions.
My first innovation is the Weighted Operational Health Index (WOHI), which combines on-schedule completion and departure regularity into one indicator, making operational performance easier to compare over time. I then use Z-scores to measure how far current performance deviates from historical norms, helping distinguish unusual disruptions from ordinary fluctuations. A Random Forest model learns patterns from historical operational and weather data to predict future operational health. Finally, Monte Carlo simulation samples different weather and operational-pressure conditions to explore a range of possible outcomes rather than relying on a single prediction. Together, these methods provide complementary views of current anomalies, future trends, and uncertainty.
Beyond the algorithms, I designed the assistant to connect its assessments to underlying data and operational rules. It separates confirmed facts from assumptions and unknowns, prioritizes verification and escalation steps, and generates different messages for operations staff, airport coordination teams, and passengers. Human feedback, including rejected recommendations and conflicting data, is recorded to guide reviewed improvements.
I plan to evaluate the system using prediction error, anomaly-detection quality, evidence traceability, recommendation accuracy, and processing time, comparing it with existing workflows on the same test scenarios. I recognize that missing data, changing measurement definitions, and unfamiliar situations can reduce reliability. Therefore, the assistant supports rather than replaces authorized human decisions, and its effectiveness must be demonstrated through testing.
